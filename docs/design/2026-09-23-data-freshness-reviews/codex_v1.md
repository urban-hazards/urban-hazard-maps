The fix resolves the selection mismatch, but leaves a misleading completeness claim and changes what the source monitor measures. All five health tests pass; they do not establish those semantics.

1. **Population:** The union is appropriate for the layer’s **latest observed report date**. A type-only chip cannot describe a map that includes queue matches. However, silently redefining `ckan_legacy:Encampments` obscures the specific type feed’s failure. Preserve separate type and queue diagnostics while computing the layer date from their union. Also, raw population does not exactly equal rendered population: [cleaner.py](/Users/brian/urban-hazard-maps/pipeline/src/pipeline/cleaner.py:32) rejects invalid coordinates. A later rejected row can advance health without reaching the map.

2. **Honesty:** “Data through June 22” beside “last complete date” implies coverage that three isolated reports cannot establish. Neither June 22 nor May 27 is demonstrably a completeness boundary. Use **“Latest report: Jun 22, 2026 — coverage incomplete”**, with an explicit explanation that the Encampments type stopped May 27 and three queue-matched June reports remain. Show the volume collapse as a second signal; moving the date alone is insufficient. Status color does not explain missing coverage, and September’s rolling counts no longer describe June’s collapse.

3. **Classification changes:** On September 23, nothing changes: zero recent reports still means stale. In other windows, changes are realistic. A recent queue report resets `days_since`, potentially changing stale to degraded—or to ok when the ratio is at least 0.5 or the baseline is absent. Because any ok source makes the layer ok, queue activity can suppress the warning while the type feed remains dead. More prior-year queue rows lower the ratio **holding current counts fixed**; current counts also expand, so the net effect can go either way. The union baseline is appropriate for union volume, but historical source metrics now have a changed definition.

4. **Years/timezones:** June remains visible throughout 2026 and 2027 because entire selected year files are scanned. I reproduced its disappearance on **January 30, 2028**, when 2026 leaves `years`: CKAN `through` becomes empty, and the supplied Open311 history makes the layer regress to May 27. This is pre-existing; compute historical latest dates independently of rolling-window years. `_local_day` shares the map cleaner’s UTC-to-Boston parser, so this change introduces no timezone divergence. For example, `2026-06-22 02:00:00` correctly becomes June 21 under the legacy UTC convention. Commit `28ec0fa` relabels Creatio timestamps during normalization; that exception should not be applied to legacy queue rows.

5. **Clipping:** Alternative A is worse for these records. Clipping to May 27 discards legitimate observations without proving earlier completeness. Clipping to the corrected June 22 cutoff changes nothing today. Retain the June reports and visibly mark their period as incomplete. Their Open status supports retaining them as reports; it does not establish complete reporting or independently verify current encampment conditions.

6. **Test confidence:** The regression test establishes inclusion of a queue row, but its September stale assertion never exercises changed classification. Both fixture rows lack coordinates and are rejected by the actual map cleaner, so the test does not establish banner/map agreement. Add a valid-coordinate integration case through point generation and health calculation; mixed current/prior-year type-and-queue fixtures asserting counts, ratio, and status; the January 2028 boundary; and near-midnight timestamp cases. Explicitly test the intended treatment of newer rows rejected by map cleaning.

7. **Fallback:** June 22 is defensible as a static latest-known-report fallback, subject to the wording correction. It has **no effect when health contains any layers**, even if encampments is missing or its date is stale. Deploying `index.astro` alone therefore cannot fix the live chip: regenerate `metadata/source_health.json` and let its frontend cache expire. The hardcoded date will diverge again if later reports arrive.
